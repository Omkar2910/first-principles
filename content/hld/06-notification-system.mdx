<Problem>
Every product eventually needs notifications: "Your order shipped", "Someone liked your post", password resets, marketing campaigns. Each goes out via push, email, SMS or in-app, through third-party providers that are slow, rate-limited and sometimes down. Users get annoyed by duplicates and spam. Design a **central notification service** that every team can use.
</Problem>

## 1. Requirements

**Functional**: other services request notifications (to a user, via one or more channels); templates with localization; user preferences and opt-outs; scheduling; in-app inbox; delivery status tracking.
**Non-functional**: 10M+ notifications/day with bursts (campaigns: 50M in an hour); transactional messages (OTP, password reset) fast and prioritized; no duplicates; at-least-once delivery for important messages; respect provider rate limits.

## 2. High-level design

```text
Producers (order svc, social svc, marketing) ──▶ Notification API  (validate, idempotency key)
                                                  │
                                                  ▼
                                    Notification service
                       ├─ load user preferences / contact info / quiet hours
                       ├─ render template (locale, variables)
                       ├─ dedupe + per-user rate limits ("max 3 marketing pushes/day")
                       └─ enqueue per channel & priority
                                                  │
              ┌────────────────┬──────────────────┼────────────────┐
              ▼                ▼                  ▼                ▼
        push queue (hi/lo) email queue        SMS queue       in-app store
              │                │                  │                │
        push workers      email workers      SMS workers       (WebSocket/SSE
        → APNs / FCM      → SES/SendGrid     → Twilio            or poll)
              │
        delivery events (sent / failed / bounced / opened) ──▶ status store + analytics
```

## 3. Key design points

**Separate queues per channel and priority.** An email provider outage shouldn't delay OTP SMS. A 50M-message marketing blast shouldn't delay password resets. Use high- and low-priority queues and dedicated workers.

**Idempotency end to end.** Producers send an idempotency key (`order-9001-shipped`); the service dedupes. Workers pass provider-level idempotency keys where supported. Users really notice duplicate notifications.

**Preferences and compliance.** Check opt-outs per channel and category before sending; honor quiet hours and time zones; include unsubscribe links (legally required for marketing email in many jurisdictions); never send marketing to users who opted out.

**Provider rate limits and retries.** Workers use token buckets per provider; retry transient errors with backoff + jitter; route to a **fallback provider** if one is down (e.g. secondary SMS vendor); dead-letter after N attempts.

**Templates.** Stored and versioned centrally (with localization); rendered by the service so product teams just send `{template: "order_shipped", vars: {...}}`.

**Fan-out for campaigns.** "Notify all 50M users" shouldn't be one request; a campaign job pages through the audience in batches and enqueues at a controlled rate.

**Tracking.** Provider webhooks (delivered, bounced, opened) update status; hard bounces disable the address; analytics measure engagement.

<MentalModel>
A **post office sorting center**. Letters (requests) arrive from many senders; clerks check the recipient's forwarding and "no junk mail" instructions (preferences), put them in the right envelope and language (templates), and sort them into **separate bins for express and standard, by carrier** (channel/priority queues). Each carrier (provider) picks up at its own pace; undeliverable letters go to a dead-letter office.
</MentalModel>

<SeniorLens>
Notification systems fail **socially** before they fail technically: duplicate pushes, 3am alerts, spam that drives uninstalls. Seniors design **frequency caps, quiet hours and deduplication** as first-class features, and give product teams guardrails rather than raw "send" power.
</SeniorLens>

<KeyTakeaways>
- Central service: API → preferences/templates/dedupe → per-channel, per-priority queues → provider workers.
- Isolate transactional from bulk traffic.
- Idempotency, retries with backoff, provider failover, DLQs.
- Preferences, quiet hours, unsubscribe and compliance are core requirements.
</KeyTakeaways>
