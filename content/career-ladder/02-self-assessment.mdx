<Problem>
"I want to be senior" isn't a plan. "I consistently operate at senior level in system design and debugging, but at mid level in written communication and cross-team influence" **is** the start of one. Honest self-assessment turns vague ambition into specific skills to practice, specific lessons to study, and specific opportunities to ask your manager for.
</Problem>

## How to rate yourself honestly

- Rate what you do **consistently**, not your best day. One great design doc doesn't make you senior in writing.
- Use **evidence**: "Which concrete examples from the last 6 months prove this?"
- It's normal to be **uneven**: strong technically, weaker in communication, or vice versa.
- Compare against the **next level's** descriptions, not against colleagues.
- Re-take it every quarter and see what moved.

## Turning gaps into a plan

For your top **2–3 gaps** (not all of them; focus wins):

1. **Learn**: study the linked lesson(s) and one external resource.
2. **Practice**: find a real opportunity at work to exercise the skill. Examples:
   - *Written communication gap* → write the design doc for your next feature, even if not required.
   - *Production ownership gap* → join the on-call rotation; write a runbook; lead the next postmortem.
   - *Influence gap* → propose one improvement that affects another team and drive it to adoption.
   - *Estimation gap* → estimate every task this quarter, track actuals, review the misses.
3. **Get feedback**: tell your manager what you're working on and ask for specific feedback and stretch opportunities.
4. **Collect evidence**: log outcomes in your brag document (lesson 18).

```text
Growth plan: Q4
Target: Senior (SDE3)
Gap 1: Written communication (Mid → Senior)
  Learn: Career lesson 7 (design docs); read 3 great design docs from our org
  Practice: write design doc for the notification-retry project; get review from Staff eng
  Evidence: doc approved, decisions referenced by 2 teams
Gap 2: Cross-team influence (Mid → Senior)
  Practice: drive adoption of shared idempotency library with payments + orders teams
  Evidence: adopted by 2 teams; duplicate-charge incidents → 0
Check-in: monthly with manager
```

<MentalModel>
Self-assessment is like a **fitness assessment before training**. You don't train everything at once; you find the weakest links (maybe flexibility, maybe endurance), work on them deliberately, and measure again later. Training only your strengths feels good but doesn't move the overall score much.
</MentalModel>

<KeyTakeaways>
- Rate consistent behavior, backed by evidence; expect unevenness.
- Pick 2–3 gaps; for each: learn, practice on real work, get feedback, collect evidence.
- Share your plan with your manager; re-assess quarterly.
</KeyTakeaways>
