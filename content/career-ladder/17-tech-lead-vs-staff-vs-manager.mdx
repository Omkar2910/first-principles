<Problem>
After senior, the ladder forks, and the names are confusing. "Tech lead" is sometimes a role and sometimes a title. "Staff engineer" means different things at different companies. Many great engineers become managers because it seemed like the only way up, and are unhappy. Understanding the **paths beyond senior** lets you choose deliberately rather than drift.
</Problem>

## The three common paths

| | **Tech Lead (TL)** | **Staff / Principal Engineer** | **Engineering Manager (EM)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary focus** | Technical execution of one team | Technical direction across teams/org | People, team health and delivery |
| **Scope** | A team (5–10 engineers) | Multiple teams, a domain or the org | A team (or several, for senior EMs) |
| **Typical work** | Design, planning, reviews, unblocking, some coding | Strategy, architecture, cross-team problems, mentoring seniors, deep technical work on critical issues | 1:1s, hiring, performance, career growth, process, stakeholder management |
| **Success measured by** | Team delivers well-designed systems predictably | Org makes better technical decisions; hard problems solved | Team is healthy, growing, and delivering |
| **Reports** | None (usually) | None | Yes |
| **Coding time** | Moderate | Varies widely (often low but deep) | Low to none |

Note: "Tech lead" is often a **role** a senior or staff engineer takes on, not a level. Staff and principal are **levels** on the IC (individual contributor) track, parallel to manager levels.

<MentalModel>
In an orchestra: the **tech lead** is the **section leader** (first violin) who plays and makes sure their section sounds great together. The **staff engineer** is like the **composer/arranger**, shaping how all sections fit and solving the hardest musical problems. The **manager** is the **orchestra's director of people**: hiring musicians, helping them grow, keeping morale and logistics working. All three are leadership; they lead different things.
</MentalModel>

## Staff engineer archetypes (Will Larson's framing)

- **Tech lead**: guides the approach and execution of a particular team.
- **Architect**: responsible for the direction and quality of a critical area across teams.
- **Solver**: parachutes into the hardest problems wherever they are.
- **Right hand**: extends an executive's attention, operating across the org.

Most staff engineers are a mix, weighted toward one.

## Choosing your path

Ask yourself honestly:
- Do I get energy from **people's growth** (1:1s, feedback, hiring, conflict resolution) or mostly from **technical problems**?
- Am I comfortable with **less coding** and more meetings?
- Do I want my impact measured through **people outcomes** or **technical outcomes**?
- What do I enjoy on my best days at work?

It's not one-way: many engineers do a **manager stint** and return to IC (the "pendulum"), often becoming better staff engineers because they understand the organization deeply. Try the waters with a tech lead role, mentoring, or leading a hiring loop.

## What doesn't change

All three require: strong technical judgment, clear communication, influence without relying on authority, and the ability to deliver outcomes through others.

<SeniorLens>
Whatever the path, the leap beyond senior is the same: **you're evaluated on outcomes you don't directly produce**. TLs through their team's delivery, staff engineers through the organization's technical decisions, managers through their people. Getting comfortable with that indirection is the real transition.
</SeniorLens>

<KeyTakeaways>
- Beyond senior: tech lead (team execution), staff/principal (cross-team technical direction), EM (people and team health).
- Tech lead is often a role; staff and EM are distinct tracks with parallel seniority.
- Choose by what energizes you; trying roles and switching back is normal.
- All paths require judgment, communication and delivering through others.
</KeyTakeaways>
