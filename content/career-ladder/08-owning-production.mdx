<Problem>
"My PR merged, so my work is done." That's mid-level thinking. Seniors know the work **starts** being real in production: under real load, real data and real failures. A senior engineer **owns a system**: its reliability, performance, cost and evolution. When it pages at 3am, they know how it behaves, and they've usually already fixed the thing that would have paged.
</Problem>

## What production ownership means

- **You know your system's health at a glance**: dashboards, SLOs, error budget, top failure modes.
- **You're on-call capable**: you can diagnose and mitigate incidents in your area and lead the response.
- **You improve operability**: alerts that matter, runbooks, safe deploys, graceful degradation.
- **You think about failure before it happens**: production readiness reviews, capacity, dependencies.
- **You close the loop after incidents**: blameless postmortems with action items that actually get done.
- **You care about cost and performance** as features of the system.

<MentalModel>
A mid-level engineer is a **builder who delivers a house**. A senior engineer is the **building's owner and caretaker**: they know which pipes are old, check the roof before storm season, keep the maintenance log, and when something leaks at night, they know where the shutoff valve is.
</MentalModel>

## Habits that build production ownership

- **Read your dashboards weekly**, even when nothing is wrong. Notice trends (latency creeping up, error budget burning slowly, cost rising) before they become incidents.
- **Take on-call seriously**: after each shift, fix the noisiest alert or write the missing runbook.
- **Ask "how will I know if this breaks?"** for every change you ship (logs, metrics, alerts).
- **Lead a postmortem**, even for a small incident.
- **Run a production readiness review** for the next launch in your area (Track 1, lesson 14).
- **Know the dependencies**: what happens to your system when each one is slow or down?
- **Learn the cost**: what does your service cost per month and per request? What drives it?

## Showing it in promotion terms

Production ownership produces strong evidence:
- "Reduced p99 latency of checkout from 1.2s to 400ms by fixing N+1 queries and adding caching."
- "Cut pages for the orders service from 30/month to 4 by removing noisy alerts and fixing two root causes."
- "Led incident response for the Oct 3 outage; postmortem actions eliminated config-change incidents."
- "Defined SLOs for search; error-budget policy adopted by the team."

<SeniorLens>
One of the clearest senior signals is being **the person others go to** when the system misbehaves, because you've built a deep understanding of how it works in production. That reputation is earned through dozens of small acts of ownership: fixed alerts, written runbooks, debugged mysteries and well-run postmortems.
</SeniorLens>

<KeyTakeaways>
- Shipping is the start; owning the system's behavior in production is the job.
- Know health, SLOs, failure modes, dependencies and cost.
- Improve operability continuously: alerts, runbooks, readiness reviews, postmortems.
- Turn ownership into measurable outcomes for your promotion evidence.
</KeyTakeaways>
