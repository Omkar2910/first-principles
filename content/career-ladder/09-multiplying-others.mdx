<Problem>
A brilliant engineer who ships a lot but whose knowledge lives only in their head, who leaves curt review comments, and who never helps newcomers is a **bottleneck**, not a senior. Senior engineers create impact beyond their own output by making everyone around them more effective. The math is simple: helping five teammates be 20% better beats being 50% better yourself.
</Problem>

## Code review that teaches

Good reviews catch bugs **and** grow people:
- **Prioritize**: correctness, security, design and maintainability over style nits (let linters handle formatting).
- **Label severity**: "**blocking**: this can double-charge on retry", "**suggestion**: consider extracting this", "**nit**: naming".
- **Explain why**, and link to docs or examples: "We use idempotency keys here because SQS redelivers (see payments design doc)."
- **Ask questions** instead of commanding: "What happens if this list is empty?" invites thinking.
- **Praise specifics**: "Nice use of a sealed interface here; it makes the states really clear."
- **Be timely**: a fast review is a gift; a 4-day wait blocks someone.
- **Pair or call** when a review becomes a long thread.

<MentalModel>
A senior engineer is like a **good coach on a sports team**. They still play, but their biggest contribution is making every other player better: showing techniques, giving specific feedback, building confidence, and creating a team that wins even when the coach isn't on the field.
</MentalModel>

## Mentoring

- **Regular 1:1 time** with mentees (even 30 min every two weeks).
- **Ask before telling**: "What have you tried? What do you think is happening?" builds problem-solving rather than dependency.
- **Give stretch work with support**: let them own something slightly beyond their comfort zone, with you as a safety net.
- **Share context**, not just answers: why the system is the way it is, how decisions get made, who to talk to.
- **Sponsor**: mention their good work in public forums and to your manager. Visibility is a huge career accelerator, especially for underrepresented engineers.

## Scaling knowledge

- **Write it down**: onboarding guides, architecture overviews, runbooks, ADRs. Every question you answer twice deserves a doc.
- **Teach in groups**: lunch-and-learns, design review walkthroughs, incident review sessions.
- **Improve the project instructions file** for AI agents and humans alike.
- **Make the right thing easy**: templates, shared libraries, linters, CI checks. Encode your knowledge in tools.
- **Onboard new hires well**: a great first month (a starter task that ships in week 1, a buddy, a map of the system) pays off for years.

<SeniorLens>
In promotion discussions, "**makes the team better**" needs evidence just like technical work: "Mentored two engineers through their first on-call; both now lead incidents." "Wrote the service onboarding guide; ramp-up time for new hires dropped from 6 to 3 weeks." "Introduced the PR template and review guidelines adopted across the org." Track these outcomes.
</SeniorLens>

<KeyTakeaways>
- Senior impact = your output + how much better you make others.
- Reviews: prioritize, label severity, explain why, ask questions, be timely, praise specifics.
- Mentor by asking, stretching and sponsoring.
- Scale knowledge with docs, talks, templates and tooling.
</KeyTakeaways>
