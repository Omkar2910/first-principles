<Problem>
Every important artifact in an engineer's career (design doc, ADR, postmortem, status update, growth plan) is easier and better when you start from a good structure instead of a blank page. Here they are in one place. Copy them, adapt them to your company, and keep the structure in mind even when you write informally.
</Problem>

## Design doc

```markdown
# <Title>
Author: · Reviewers: · Status: Draft | In review | Approved · Updated:

## TL;DR
One paragraph: problem, proposal, key tradeoff, ask.

## Context & problem
What's happening and why it matters now (with data).

## Goals / Non-goals

## Proposed design
Architecture diagram · data flow · APIs · data model · key algorithms

## Alternatives considered
Option → why not chosen

## Tradeoffs & risks
Risk → mitigation

## Operations
SLOs · monitoring/alerts · failure modes · rollout & rollback · migration/backfill

## Security, privacy & compliance

## Cost
Expected monthly cost now and at 10× scale; main drivers

## Open questions

## Milestones
```

## Architecture Decision Record (ADR)

```markdown
# ADR-NNN: <Decision title>
Status: Proposed | Accepted | Superseded by ADR-MMM · Date: · Deciders:

## Context
Forces at play, constraints, the problem.

## Decision
What we decided, stated plainly.

## Consequences
Positive, negative, and follow-ups.

## Alternatives considered
```

## Postmortem

```markdown
# <Incident title>: <date>
Severity: · Duration: · Authors: · Status: Draft | Reviewed

## Summary
2–3 sentences: what happened, impact, resolution.

## Impact
Users/customers affected · duration · business impact · data impact

## Timeline (UTC)
hh:mm event (detection, escalation, key decisions, mitigation, resolution)

## Root cause & contributing factors
Technical · process · organizational

## What went well · What went poorly · Where we got lucky

## Action items
| Action | Type (prevent/detect/mitigate) | Owner | Due | Ticket |
```

## Project status update (weekly)

```markdown
**<Project>**: week of <date> · Status: 🟢 On track | 🟡 At risk | 🔴 Off track
**Done:** ...
**Next:** ...
**Risks / blockers:** risk → mitigation / help needed from whom
**Decisions needed:** question → by when → from whom
**Milestone dates:** M2 (Oct 20, on track) · Launch (Nov 15, at risk: reason)
```

## 1:1 agenda (with your manager)

```markdown
## Wins since last time
## What I'm working on / blocked by
## Feedback (both directions)
## Growth: progress on my plan; opportunities I'd like
## Anything else (team, process, ideas, concerns)
```

## Growth plan

```markdown
# Growth plan: <period>
Target level: · Mentor/manager:

| Gap (competency) | Current → target | Learn | Practice (real work) | Evidence of progress | Check-in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Written communication | Mid → Senior | Career lesson 7 | Design doc for X | Approved; reused by team Y | Monthly |
```

## Brag document

```markdown
# Brag doc: <period>
## Goals
## Projects (impact, numbers, links)
## Production & operations
## Collaboration, mentoring & glue work
## Learning & sharing
## Feedback received
```

## Production readiness checklist (short)

```markdown
- [ ] Health: startup/liveness/readiness probes (liveness independent of deps)
- [ ] Graceful shutdown; config validated at startup
- [ ] Timeouts + retry policy on every outbound call; circuit breakers for critical deps
- [ ] Resource requests/limits; JVM sized to container
- [ ] RED metrics, structured logs with request IDs, tracing
- [ ] SLOs + burn-rate alerts with runbooks and an owner
- [ ] Load tested to forecast peak (with N−1 capacity)
- [ ] Backups + tested restore; backward-compatible migrations
- [ ] AuthN/AuthZ on every endpoint; secrets from manager; deps scanned
- [ ] Rollout plan (canary/flags) and rollback tested
```

<MentalModel>
Templates are **checklists for thinking**, like a pilot's pre-flight checklist. Experienced pilots still use them, not because they can't remember, but because under pressure everyone forgets something. A template makes sure the important questions get asked every time.
</MentalModel>

<KeyTakeaways>
- Start important documents from proven structures.
- Adapt templates to your company, but keep the core questions.
- Lead with the TL;DR; make action items owned and dated; keep updates short and honest.
</KeyTakeaways>
