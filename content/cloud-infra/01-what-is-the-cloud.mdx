<Problem>
"Deploy it to the cloud" hides a lot of decisions: which region? One availability zone or three? A VM, a container platform, or a serverless function? Who patches the OS? Who's responsible when data leaks through a misconfigured bucket? The cloud is **someone else's datacenters exposed through APIs**, and using it well means understanding its building blocks and its failure domains.
</Problem>

## Physical structure: regions and availability zones

- **Region**: a geographic area (e.g. `us-east-1`, `ap-south-1` Mumbai) containing multiple datacenters. Choose by **user latency**, **data residency laws** and **service availability/price**.
- **Availability Zone (AZ)**: one or more datacenters within a region with independent power, cooling and networking, a few km apart, connected by low-latency links (~1–2 ms).
- **Edge locations**: CDN/DNS points of presence, many more than regions.

The fundamental reliability pattern: **spread across AZs** (survive a datacenter failure with little latency cost). **Multi-region** survives a regional outage but brings data replication and consistency challenges (Track 4).

<MentalModel>
A region is a **city**; AZs are **separate neighborhoods** on different power grids in that city. If a storm knocks out one neighborhood, the others keep running, and they're close enough to commute between in a minute. Moving to a different city (multi-region) protects against a city-wide disaster, but everything becomes farther apart.
</MentalModel>

## Service models: how much do you manage?

| Model | You manage | Provider manages | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **On-prem** | Everything | Nothing | Your own servers |
| **IaaS** | OS, runtime, app, scaling | Hardware, virtualization, network | EC2, Compute Engine, Azure VMs |
| **CaaS / managed K8s** | Containers, app, cluster config | Control plane, nodes (partly) | EKS, GKE, ECS/Fargate, Cloud Run |
| **PaaS** | App code + config | Runtime, OS, scaling | Elastic Beanstalk, Heroku, App Engine |
| **Serverless / FaaS** | Functions | Everything else, including scaling to zero | Lambda, Cloud Functions |
| **SaaS / managed services** | Usage and data | The whole service | RDS, S3, SQS, DynamoDB, Auth0 |

Moving down the table trades **control** for **less operational work**. Most teams should use managed services for undifferentiated heavy lifting (databases, queues, object storage) and spend engineering time on their product.

## The shared responsibility model

The provider secures **the cloud** (physical security, hypervisors, managed service internals). You secure **what you put in the cloud**: IAM permissions, network rules, encryption settings, OS patches on your VMs, your application, your data. Public S3 buckets and over-permissive IAM roles, both customer-side misconfigurations, cause most cloud breaches.

## Cloud economics in one paragraph

You pay for what you provision (VMs, storage) or use (requests, GB transferred). Watch for: **data egress** (transfer out to the internet or across regions/AZs is often the surprise bill), idle resources, over-provisioned instances, and chatty cross-AZ traffic. Savings levers: autoscaling, right-sizing, reserved/savings plans for steady load, spot instances for interruptible work, storage tiering.

<SeniorLens>
Seniors default to **"managed service unless proven otherwise"**: running your own Kafka or Postgres on VMs is a full-time job. They also read the bill. Cost is an architectural property, and a design that's 3× more expensive than needed is a design flaw.
</SeniorLens>

<KeyTakeaways>
- Regions for geography and compliance; AZs for fault isolation. Multi-AZ by default.
- IaaS → containers → PaaS → serverless → managed services: less control, less ops.
- Shared responsibility: you own IAM, network rules, data and app security.
- Watch egress, idle and over-provisioned resources.
</KeyTakeaways>
