<Problem>
A team runs 12 always-on servers for an internal tool used 2 hours a day. Another team moved a high-traffic API to Lambda and their bill tripled. A third discovers a NAT gateway is their largest line item. **Serverless** can be wonderfully cheap and simple, or the wrong tool. And in the cloud, **cost is an architectural property** you design, not an accident you discover.
</Problem>

## What "serverless" means

You deploy code or use a service without managing servers; it **scales automatically (including to zero)** and you **pay per use**. Examples: Lambda/Cloud Functions, Cloud Run/Fargate (serverless containers), DynamoDB on-demand, S3, SQS, EventBridge, Step Functions, API Gateway.

## Where it shines

- **Spiky or low/unpredictable traffic**: internal tools, webhooks, cron jobs, prototypes.
- **Event-driven glue**: "when a file lands in S3, generate a thumbnail"; "when a message arrives, process it".
- **Scale-to-zero** environments (preview/dev environments).
- Small teams that want minimal operations.

## Sharp edges

- **Cold starts**: the first request to a new instance waits for initialization (JVM especially; mitigate with SnapStart, GraalVM native images, provisioned concurrency, or lighter runtimes).
- **Limits**: execution time (Lambda max 15 min), payload sizes, concurrency quotas.
- **Downstream overload**: 1,000 concurrent functions can open 1,000 DB connections. Use RDS Proxy/pools and concurrency limits.
- **Cost at sustained high load**: per-request pricing beats servers at low utilization, but **steady heavy traffic is usually cheaper on containers/VMs**.
- **Local testing and observability** take extra effort; logic spread across many functions can become hard to follow.

<MentalModel>
Serverless is **ride-hailing**; servers are **owning a car**. For occasional trips, ride-hailing is cheaper and you never think about maintenance. If you drive 4 hours every day, owning a car wins on cost, though now you handle insurance and oil changes. Many people use both, depending on the trip.
</MentalModel>

## Cost engineering: the main levers

| Driver | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Idle capacity** | Autoscaling, scale-to-zero for non-prod, shut down dev at night |
| **Over-provisioning** | Right-size from real utilization (most instances run at 10–20% CPU) |
| **Commitment discounts** | Savings Plans / Reserved Instances for steady baseline load |
| **Spot / preemptible** | Batch jobs, CI runners, stateless workers (up to ~70–90% cheaper) |
| **Data transfer** | Watch egress to internet, cross-region and cross-AZ traffic; use CDNs and VPC endpoints |
| **Storage** | Lifecycle policies (S3 → Infrequent Access → Glacier), delete orphaned snapshots/volumes |
| **Observability costs** | Logs and metrics cardinality can explode; sample, set retention, drop noise |
| **Managed service pricing models** | On-demand vs provisioned (DynamoDB), per-request vs per-hour |

**FinOps** practices: tag every resource with owner/service/env, show teams their own costs, set budgets and anomaly alerts, and review cost in design docs ("this design costs ~$X/month at expected load").

## Unit economics

Track **cost per business unit**: cost per order, per active user, per 1,000 API calls. It tells you whether growth is efficient and makes cost a product conversation ("this feature costs $0.02 per use in LLM calls").

<SeniorLens>
Seniors include a **cost section in every design doc**: expected monthly cost at launch and at 10× scale, the biggest cost drivers, and how cost scales with usage. It's frequently the section leadership reads most carefully, and it catches designs where cost grows faster than revenue.
</SeniorLens>

<KeyTakeaways>
- Serverless = no servers, auto-scale, pay per use; great for spiky and event-driven work.
- Watch cold starts, limits, downstream connections and cost at steady high load.
- Main cost levers: idle, right-sizing, commitments, spot, data transfer, storage tiers, observability.
- Track unit economics; put cost in design docs.
</KeyTakeaways>
